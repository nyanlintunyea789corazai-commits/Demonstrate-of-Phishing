Phishing Awareness Demo

This lesson shows students how a fake login page steals usernames and passwords, and how to spot one. It uses a fictional bank ("ExampleBank") and works fully offline: no form is submitted to any server, and the "captured" details are kept only in the open browser tab until you erase them or close it.

Start the demo: open the fake login page

Suggested lesson flow (about 15 minutes)

  1. Set the scene. Tell the class: "You got a text saying your bank account is locked. You tapped the link."
  2. Open the fake page on the projector. Ask: "Would you sign in here?" Let students point out anything odd before you sign in.
  3. Sign in with a made-up test account (for example student@example.com / Summer2025!). Never use a real password.
  4. Reveal the debrief. Walk through what was captured, especially the details nobody typed.
  5. Go through the red flags one by one and see how many the class caught.
  6. Finish with the protection tips, then press Erase captured data.

Tip: the password on the debrief page is masked by default so it isn't shown on the projector by accident. Press Show when you want to reveal it.

Discussion questions

  • Which red flag would you be most likely to miss on a phone? Why?
  • Why does a padlock (HTTPS) not prove a site is safe?
  • How would a password manager have protected you here?
  • If you reuse one password on five sites and it gets phished, how many accounts are at risk?
  • Why are passkeys and security keys resistant to this attack?

How to run it

Double-click index.html to open it in any modern browser. No install or internet connection needed.
